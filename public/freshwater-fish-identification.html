Freshwater fish identification by body shape

Body shape is the fastest first filter when the chart does not give an instant match, because it reflects how the fish feeds and where it lives:

  • Fusiform (streamlined): trout, salmon, bass, pike. Built for speed in open water or current.
  • Laterally compressed (deep-bodied): sunfish, crappie, bluegill, perch, bream. Manoeuvrable in weedy or structured habitat.
  • Depressed (flattened top-to-bottom): catfish, sturgeon. Bottom-dwelling; mouth usually positioned below the snout.
  • Elongated (eel-like or pike-like): gar, bowfin, pike, muskellunge. Ambush predators in slow or shallow water.
  • Oval, thick-lipped (cichlid): oscar, tilapia, peacock bass. Long single dorsal fin, often with an eye-spot near the tail.

Freshwater fish identification FAQ

How do I tell a largemouth bass from a smallmouth bass?

Close the mouth and look at the upper jaw. On a largemouth it reaches past the rear edge of the eye; on a smallmouth it stops below the eye. Smallmouth also show vertical bars and a bronze tone, while largemouth carry a dark horizontal stripe.

How do I tell a northern pike from a muskellunge?

Pike have light spots on a dark body; muskie have dark bars or spots on a light body. Pike tails are rounded at the lobe tips, muskie tails are pointed, and the muskie has fewer than six pores under each side of the jaw versus more on a pike.

Carp or goldfish?

Common carp have two pairs of barbels at the mouth; goldfish have none. Large wild goldfish are olive-bronze and are regularly mistaken for carp on that basis alone.
