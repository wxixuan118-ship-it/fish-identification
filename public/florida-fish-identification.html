Florida Fish Identification: From the Panhandle to the Keys

Florida's waters span the Gulf of Mexico, the Atlantic coast, and the unique subtropical ecosystem of the Florida Keys — making it one of North America's most species-rich fishing destinations. Florida Saltwater Species Overview

Red SnapperLutjanus campechanus

Pink-red body; distinctive pointed anal fin; large eye. Prime offshore target; found on hard bottom structures 20–100 m.

Spotted SeatroutCynoscion nebulosus

Silver with black spots; two prominent canine teeth. The most popular inshore game fish on both coasts.

Redfish (Red Drum)Sciaenops ocellatus

Copper-bronze; one or more black spots at tail base. Inshore flats stalwart; bull reds school nearshore in fall.

SnookCentropomus undecimalis

Distinctive black lateral line on silver body; protruding lower jaw. Warm-water species concentrated in south Florida year-round.

TarponMegalops atlanticus

Large silver scales; upturned mouth; up to 2.5 m long. The "Silver King" — legendary for acrobatic jumps when hooked.

Mahi-MahiCoryphaena hippurus

Vivid green-and-gold with blue spots offshore; males have blunt foreheads. Rapid colour change at boatside is unmistakeable.

Florida Keys Specialties

The Florida Keys extend 200 km from Miami to Key West, with the only living coral barrier reef in the continental United States running along their Atlantic side. Unique species and sightings:

  • Permit (Trachinotus falcatus): Deep-bodied silver fish with a black-edged tail; feeds on crabs on the flats — the holy grail for Keys fly fishing.
  • Bonefish (Albula vulpes): Extremely fast; silver and almost transparent. Tails on shallow flats make for legendary sight-fishing.
  • Goliath Grouper (Epinephelus itajara): Can exceed 400 kg; brownish with dark spots. Frequently seen by divers around nearshore wrecks and ledges.
  • Cubera Snapper (Lutjanus cyanopterus): Largest Atlantic snapper; deep red-brown; found around reef ledges and wrecks.
  • Hogfish (Lachnolaimus maximus): Long pig-like snout; pink and white; common on reef structures — a prized eating fish.

Panhandle & Gulf Coast ID Notes

The northwestern Florida Gulf Coast (Pensacola, Destin, Tampa Bay) has cooler, less tropical water and a slightly different species mix:

  • King and Spanish mackerel are common nearshore in warmer months.
  • Cobia (Rachycentron canadum): Dark brown with a white belly stripe; often seen following rays on the surface — one of the most distinctive inshore pelagics.
  • Flounder (Gulf Flounder, Southern Flounder): Flat, camouflaged bottom fish; the eyes are on the left side of the body in Gulf flounder.
  • Sheepshead (Archosargus probatocephalus): Black and white vertical stripes; human-like teeth; found around pilings and structure — one of the trickiest fish to identify by name alone.
