Gulf of Mexico Fish Identification: Species Guide

The Gulf of Mexico covers 1.6 million km² and supports one of the world's most productive fisheries. Its mix of shallow bays, seagrass flats, deep canyon walls, and offshore oil platforms creates an extraordinary range of habitats — and fish. This guide covers the Gulf's most frequently encountered species from the Texas coast to the Florida panhandle.

Nearshore & Inshore Gulf Species

Speckled TroutCynoscion nebulosus

Black spots on silver body; two canine teeth visible. The most popular inshore target across all Gulf states.

RedfishSciaenops ocellatus

Copper-coloured with a distinctive black spot (or spots) at the tail base. Schools in the surf zone during fall spawning runs.

FlounderParalichthys albigutta

Flat, camouflaged; lies on sandy bottom. Both eyes are on one side. Common gigging target in shallow bays at night.

SheepsheadArchosargus probatocephalus

Bold black and white stripes; human-like molar teeth for crushing shells. Loves pilings, jetties, and oyster beds.

Black DrumPogonias cromis

High-backed; barbels (whiskers) under chin; vertical bars fade with age. Makes a distinctive drumming sound.

CobiaRachycentron canadum

Dark brown dorsally; white belly with a lateral brown stripe. Often seen following rays, turtles, or sharks on the surface.

Offshore Gulf Species

Red SnapperLutjanus campechanus

Iconic Gulf fish; rose-red with a pointed anal fin and large eye. Found on hard bottom from 20–100 m. One of the Gulf's most prized table fish.

AmberjackSeriola dumerili

Amber stripe from eye to tail; strong, hard-fighting pelagic. Found around offshore platforms and wrecks — can exceed 60 kg.

King MackerelScomberomorus cavalla

Streamlined silver; lateral line dips sharply at mid-body. Distinguished from Spanish mackerel by the absence of gold spots.

Warsaw GrouperHyporthodus nigritus

Dark brown; 10 dorsal spines (one more than other groupers). Deep-water species; one of the largest Atlantic groupers.

Yellowfin TunaThunnus albacares

Bright yellow fins and finlets; elongated second dorsal and anal fins in larger fish. Deepwater offshore pelagic.

WahooAcanthocybium solandri

Elongated; blue-grey with vertical blue bars; knife-like teeth. Among the fastest fish in the ocean — can exceed 80 km/h.

Texas Saltwater Fish Notes

Texas has over 1,000 km of Gulf coastline and a distinct estuarine ecosystem in its bays and lagoons (Galveston Bay, Matagorda Bay, Corpus Christi Bay, Laguna Madre). Key Texas species:

  • Spotted Seatrout & Redfish: The cornerstone of Texas inshore fishing year-round; strict slot limits protect breeding fish.
  • Flounder: Both Gulf and southern flounder occur; fall migration into the surf is the classic gigging season.
  • Alligator Gar (Atractosteus spatula): Ancient, armoured fish up to 3 m long; found in brackish bays and river mouths. Prehistoric appearance makes it instantly recognisable.
  • Tripletail (Lobotes surinamensis): Dark, laterally compressed; drifts on its side near floating debris, mimicking a leaf. The three rounded fin lobes at the back give it its name.
