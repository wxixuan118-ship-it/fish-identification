How to use this fish identification chart

1. Pick the habitat first. Use the All / Saltwater / Freshwater / Reef buttons above the chart. Habitat rules out more species than any single body feature, so it is the fastest first cut.
2. Compare the picture, then the traits. Colour photos are the quickest match, but colour changes with age, sex, water and death (a mahi-mahi fades in minutes). The Key ID Traits column lists shape, fin and marking features that do not change.
3. Check size and range. A fish outside its normal size range or thousands of miles from its distribution is usually a different species that looks similar.
4. Still torn between two rows? Upload the photo to the AI fish identifier and it returns the top matches with a confidence score.

Reading the chart when a fish does not match

Most misidentifications on a chart come from three things. Juveniles often wear different colours from adults (a blue tang starts life bright yellow). Regional forms of the same species vary, so treat the picture as a typical example, not a template. And dead fish lose colour: if you are identifying a catch on the dock, lean on body shape, fin shape, tooth pattern and lateral-line markings rather than hue. When two rows still fit, the AI identifier compares hundreds of features at once and is the quicker tie-breaker.

Fish identification chart FAQ

Does this chart cover aquarium fish?

This master chart is built around commonly caught and commonly seen wild species. For tank fish, the FishCare AI species database covers 250+ aquarium species with care parameters, and the photo identifier recognises both wild and aquarium fish.

Can I print the fish identification chart?

Yes. Pick a habitat filter, then print the page; the table keeps its photo, species, size and traits columns on paper. For a pocket reference on a boat, the freshwater and saltwater charts are shorter and print on fewer pages.

Which is more reliable, a chart or an AI identifier?

They answer different questions. A chart teaches you what to look at and works with no signal. The AI identifier is faster and better at separating close look-alikes, but it needs a clear side-on photo. Use the chart to narrow, the identifier to confirm.
