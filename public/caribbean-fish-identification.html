Caribbean Fish Identification Chart: 21 Species with Pictures

The Caribbean holds about 1,400 marine fish species, and its reefs belong to the Atlantic province, so almost nothing here overlaps with the Indo-Pacific. This Caribbean fish identification chart pairs a photo of each species you are most likely to meet snorkelling, diving or fishing from the Bahamas to Bonaire with the traits that separate it from its neighbours. What makes Caribbean fish identification different

  • No clownfish, one tang. Anemonefish are absent from the Atlantic. The blue tang is the only common Caribbean surgeonfish, and its juveniles are yellow.
  • Atlantic-only groupers and snappers. Nassau grouper, goliath grouper, coney, graysby, yellowtail, mutton and schoolmaster snapper occur nowhere else.
  • Wrasse and parrotfish change with age and sex. Bluehead wrasse and stoplight parrotfish have an initial phase and a terminal (male) phase that look like different species; the chart shows both where they are commonly seen.
  • Invasive lionfish. Red lionfish from the Indo-Pacific have spread across the whole region since the 1980s and are now one of the most frequently seen reef fish.

Caribbean regions and what you will see

Florida Keys and Bahamas

Shallow flats for bonefish, permit and tarpon; patch reefs with hogfish, mutton and yellowtail snapper. See Florida fish identification.

Cayman, Cozumel and Belize

Wall diving: queen and French angelfish, rock beauty, Nassau grouper on ledges, eagle rays and schools of grunts.

Bonaire, Curaçao and the ABC islands

Shore-diving reefs with dense sergeant majors, bluehead wrasse, stoplight parrotfish, trumpetfish and many lionfish.

Cuba, Jamaica and Hispaniola

Mangrove and reef mix: snook, mangrove snapper, tarpon in channels, plus the standard reef community.

Caribbean fish identification FAQ

What fish will I see snorkelling in the Caribbean?

On almost any shallow Caribbean reef you will meet sergeant majors, bluehead wrasse, French grunts, blue tangs, stoplight parrotfish and foureye butterflyfish within the first few minutes, with yellowtail snapper hovering above the coral and a barracuda watching from the edge.

Are there clownfish or tangs in the Caribbean?

No clownfish: anemonefish are Indo-Pacific only. The Caribbean has one common tang, the blue tang (Acanthurus coeruleus), which is a different species from the Pacific blue tang seen in films.

Is the lionfish native to the Caribbean?

No. The red lionfish is an Indo-Pacific species released in Florida in the 1980s and now found on every Caribbean reef. It is edible and many islands run culling programmes.
